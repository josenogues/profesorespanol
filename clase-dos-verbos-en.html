One English verb, two Spanish ones

Eight splits, one rule each — and the one that catches everybody

1. The four you'll need this week

Englishthe twohow to choose
to knowsaber / conocersaber = facts and how to do things. conocer = people and places.
Sé su nombre pero no la conozco.
to askpreguntar / pedirpreguntar = ask a question. pedir = ask for a thing.
Pregunté la hora y pedí un café.
to take / bringllevar / traerllevar = away from where you are. traer = towards where you are.
Llevo el coche al taller y te traigo el mío.
to go / comeir / venirSame idea. venir only ever means towards the person speaking.
Voy a tu casa.
The one that catches absolutely everybody: someone calls you from the kitchen and you answer ¡Voy! — not ¡Vengo!. You're going to them, so it's ir. In English you say «I'm coming» and that is exactly the trap.

2. The other four

Englishthe twohow to choose
timetiempo / hora / veztiempo = time in general (and the weather). hora = the clock. vez = an occasion.
No tengo tiempo. ¿Qué hora es? Otra vez.
to playjugar / tocarjugar a game or a sport, tocar an instrument.
Juego al tenis y toco la guitarra.
to leavedejar / salirdejar = leave something behind. salir = go out.
Dejé las llaves en casa y salí corriendo.
to worktrabajar / funcionarPeople trabajan. Machines funcionan.
El ascensor no funciona, no trabaja.

3. And «to become», which splits into three

No single Spanish verb covers it. Which one you use says how it happened.

ponerse

  • a mood, and it passes
  • Se puso triste.
  • Me pongo nervioso.

hacerse

  • with effort, over time
  • Se hizo médico.
  • Se hizo rico.

volverse

  • a change in character, and it stays
  • Se volvió insoportable.
  • Se ha vuelto muy callado.
