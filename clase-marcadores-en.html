The words that hold a story together

When it happened, and how one idea leads to the next. Without these you have a list of facts; with them you have a story.

1. Back: when it already happened

These go at the start of the sentence nine times out of ten. Say the marker, then the story.

SpanishEnglish
ayeryesterday
anteayerthe day before yesterday
anochelast night
la semana pasadalast week
el mes pasado · el año pasadolast month · last year
hace tres díasthree days ago
hace un añoa year ago
cuando era niñowhen I was a kid
una vezonce
Hace is «ago», and it goes in front, not behind. English puts it at the end — «three days ago» — and Spanish puts it at the start: hace tres días. Never tres días hace.

2. Now, and what you always do

Same slot, same job. These are the ones that let you talk about your life rather than one day of it.

SpanishEnglish
ahora · hoynow · today
actualmentecurrently
normalmenteusually
a vecessometimes
de vez en cuandofrom time to time
siempre · nuncaalways · never
todos los días · cada semanaevery day · every week
Actualmente does not mean «actually». It means «currently», right now in your life. For English «actually», you want en realidad or de hecho.

3. Forward: what has not happened yet

The mirror image of the first table. One of them is the trap.

SpanishEnglish
mañanatomorrow
pasado mañanathe day after tomorrow
la semana que vienenext week
el mes que viene · el año que vienenext month · next year
dentro de tres díasin three days
prontosoon
algún díasomeday
en el futuroin the future
The pair that matters: hace tres días = three days ago. Dentro de tres días = in three days. Same three days, opposite directions. Mix them up and you have moved the appointment by a week.

4. And now the glue: connectors

The markers say when. These say how one idea relates to the last one. Five groups, and you already use all five in English.

Adding and contrasting

  • y — and
  • también — also
  • además — besides, what is more
  • incluso — even
  • pero — but
  • sin embargo — however
  • aunque — although
  • en cambio — on the other hand

Cause, order and closing

  • porque — because
  • como — since, as (goes first)
  • por eso — that is why
  • así que — so
  • primero · luego · después — first, then, after
  • mientras tanto — meanwhile
  • al final — in the end
  • en resumen — in summary
Porque and como both mean «because», but they sit in different places. No fui porque llovía — the reason goes second. Como llovía, no fui — the reason goes first. Never start a sentence with porque.
