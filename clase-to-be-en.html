One «to be» in English, three in Spanish

ser, estar and hay — and how to tell in one second which one you need

1. The three of them, one line each

English says I am tired, I am English and there is a bar on the corner with the same verb. Spanish uses a different one for each.

what it doesexample
serwhat something is — who you are, where you're from, what it's made of, the timeSoy inglés. Es médico. Son las tres.
estarhow it is right now, and where it isEstoy cansado. La llave está en la mesa.
haythat something exists — you're mentioning it for the first timeHay un bar en la esquina. ¿Hay leche?

2. The street rule

Three questions, in this order. The first one that says yes wins.

Can a bad day change it?

  • Then it's estar.
  • Estoy cansado. Está roto. Está cerrado.

Are you saying it exists?

  • Then it's hay.
  • Hay un problema. Hay gente.

Everything else

  • Then it's ser.
  • Soy de Londres. Es grande. Es mi hermana.

3. hay or está? Look at the word in front

This is the one that really decides it, and it takes half a second: hay goes with un, una, dos, mucho, nada. está goes with el, la, mi, este.

you want to saySpanishnot
There's a bar near hereHay un bar cercaEstá un bar cerca
The bar is near hereEl bar está cercaEl bar hay cerca
There are two supermarketsHay dos supermercados
My sister is at homeMi hermana está en casa
Is there any milk?¿Hay leche?
Where's the milk?¿Dónde está la leche?

Hay never changes. One bar or twenty bars, it's always hay.

4. ser and estar side by side

serestar
who or what you areSoy profesor. Es mi hermana.
where you're fromSoy de Londres.
what it's made ofEs de madera.
the time and the dateSon las ocho. Es lunes.
how you feelEstoy cansado. Está contenta.
where it isEstoy en el bar. Madrid está en España.
what's happening nowEstá lloviendo. Está durmiendo.

Both in one sentence, which is how you'll actually use them: Soy de Italia pero estoy en España.

One odd one worth knowing: an event takes ser. La fiesta es en mi casa — the party happens there. But the house itself: Mi casa está en el centro.
