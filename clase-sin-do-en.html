There is no «do» here

Questions and negatives with nothing but the verb

1. A question is the same sentence, said upwards

English needs a helper to ask: do, does, did. Spanish needs nothing. You say the sentence and go up at the end.

EnglishSpanishnot
Do you work?¿Trabajas?¿Haces trabajar?
Does she live here?¿Vive aquí?
Did you eat?¿Comiste?
Do you have brothers or sisters?¿Tienes hermanos?

The upside-down ¿ at the start is there to tell you to lift your voice before you begin. It's a reading instruction.

2. Negative: one word, in front of the verb

No helper either. No goes before the verb and the job is done.

EnglishSpanish
I don't knowNo sé
She doesn't workNo trabaja
I didn't goNo fui
I don't like itNo me gusta

When you answer a question you need it twice, and both are doing a job: ¿Vienes? — No, no vengo. The first one is «no», the second one is «don't».

3. The double negative — compulsory here

English forbids it. Spanish asks for it. If the negative word comes after the verb, you still need no in front.

EnglishSpanish
I don't have anythingNo tengo nada
Nobody cameNo vino nadie
I never goNo voy nunca
I don't have one eitherNo tengo tampoco

Put the negative word first and the no disappears. Both versions are correct: Nadie vino. Nunca voy.

4. Two things that save you a lot of work

Question words

  • qué — what
  • quién — who
  • dónde — where
  • cuándo — when
  • cómo — how
  • por qué — why
  • cuánto — how much

Tag questions

  • English changes it every time: isn't it, don't you, didn't she.
  • Spanish has two: ¿no? and ¿verdad?
  • Hace frío, ¿no?
  • Eres inglés, ¿verdad?
In a question the question word carries an accent: ¿Dónde vives? Without it, it isn't a question any more: donde vives = «where you live».

Practice 2 — the double negative

In Spanish two negatives do not cancel out. They are compulsory together.
