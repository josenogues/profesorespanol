Verbs

How Spanish verbs work

Understanding Spanish verbs is key to fluent communication, as they convey action, state, and intention. Let's explore the fundamental building blocks of Spanish verb conjugation.

Regular vs. Irregular

Most verbs follow predictable patterns based on their ending (–AR, –ER, –IR). Common verbs like ser and ir are irregular — they must be memorized.

Three moods

Indicative (facts) — 10 verb tenses: 5 simple + 5 compound.
Subjunctive (doubts & desires) — 6 verb tenses: 3 simple + 3 compound.
Imperative (commands).

Non-finite forms

Infinitive (hablar), Gerund (hablando), Participle (hablado). These forms don't conjugate and are used in compound tenses and descriptions.

The five simple tenses — side by side

The five most useful tenses in the indicative mood, using hablar (to speak) as the example.

Pretérito Imperfecto

hablaba — I used to speak / I was speaking

For ongoing or habitual past actions.

Presente

hablo — I speak

For current or habitual actions.

Condicional Simple

hablaría — I would speak

For hypothetical or conditional actions.

Pretérito Perfecto

hablé — I spoke

For completed past actions.

Futuro Simple

hablaré — I will speak

For future actions.

The Imperfecto and Perfecto are both past tenses but work differently: Imperfecto = ongoing/habitual (hablaba), Perfecto = completed (hablé). The Condicional is like the English "would" — great for polite requests and hypotheticals.
