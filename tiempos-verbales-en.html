Verbs

Which tense do I use

A map of the verb system: which tenses live in each mood and what each one is for. This page is the when; the how — the full tables — is in Conjugaciones.

Indicative

What you take as fact: it happened, happens or will happen.

TenseAn exampleWhat it is for
PresentehabloWhat is happening now, what you always do, and what is simply true.
Pretérito perfecto simplehabléSomething finished, inside a closed moment: ayer, en 2019, el martes.
Pretérito imperfectohablabaThe scenery of the past: how things were, what used to happen, what was going on.
Futuro simplehablaréWhat will happen — and also a guess: estará en casa.
Condicional simplehablaríaWhat would happen if, and politeness: ¿podrías ayudarme?
Pretérito perfecto compuestohe habladoPast with one foot in the present: hoy, esta semana, en mi vida.
Pretérito pluscuamperfectohabía habladoA past before another past: ya había comido cuando llegaste.
Pretérito anteriorhube habladoBarely used outside literature. Recognise it and move on.
Futuro compuestohabré habladoFinished before a future moment: habré acabado para las cinco.
Condicional compuestohabría habladoWhat would have happened and didn't: habría ido, pero llovía.

Subjunctive

What you don't take as fact: you want it, doubt it, feel it or imagine it.

TenseAn exampleWhat it is for
PresentehableA wish, a doubt, an emotion or a request about something that isn't a fact yet.
Pretérito imperfectohablara / hablaseThe same, in the past or in a hypothesis: si hablara más, aprendería. Both forms are equally fine.
Futuro simplehablareA fossil: legal texts and proverbs. You never need to produce it.
Pretérito perfectohaya habladoSomething already done, seen through doubt or a wish: espero que haya llegado.
Pretérito pluscuamperfectohubiera / hubiese habladoRegret and the «what if…»: si hubiera sabido, no voy.
Futuro compuestohubiere habladoAnother fossil. You'll see it in a contract, not in a conversation.

Imperative

Giving orders and asking for things. It only has forms of its own in the affirmative —habla, hablad—; to say don't, you borrow the subjunctive: no hables.

Non-finite forms

They say neither who nor when, but without them there are no compound tenses and no periphrases: infinitive (hablar), gerund (hablando) and participle (hablado).

What is actually hard is not learning sixteen tenses: it is choosing between two. Hablé or he hablado, hablaba or hablé, indicative or subjunctive. Those three decisions are worth more than half the table.
