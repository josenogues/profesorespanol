Class

One adjective, two meanings

The dozen words where picking ser or estar changes what you just said

The first class on this: «One «to be» in English, three in Spanish»

1. Why this is worth a whole class

You already know ser is what something is and estar is how it is right now. With most adjectives that's a shade of meaning. With the dozen below it changes the word completely — and a couple of them will get you into trouble.

2. The twelve

with serwith estar
aburridoEs aburrido — he's boringEstá aburrido — he's bored
listoEs listo — he's cleverEstá listo — he's ready
ricoEs rico — he's richEstá rico — it's delicious
buenoEs bueno — he's a good personEstá bueno — it tastes good
maloEs malo — he's a bad personEstá malo — he's ill, or it's gone off
verdeEs verde — it's greenEstá verde — it's unripe
vivoEs vivo — he's sharpEstá vivo — he's alive
despiertoEs despierto — he's brightEstá despierto — he's awake
orgullosoEs orgulloso — he's arrogantEstá orgulloso — he's proud
calladoEs callado — he's a quiet personEstá callado — he's not saying anything right now
abiertoEs abierto — he's open-mindedEstá abierto — it's open
negroEs negro — it's blackEstá negro — he's furious

3. You don't have to learn them one by one

Look down the table again. The ser column is always what someone is like — their character, how they are as a person. The estar column is always the state they're in today.

ser → character

  • Es callado — a quiet sort of person
  • Es aburrido — boring, always
  • Es malo — a bad person

estar → today

  • Está callado — quiet right now, something's up
  • Está aburrido — bored this afternoon
  • Está malo — ill this week

With that in your head you can work out most of the table instead of memorising it.

4. The two that can get you in trouble

bueno

  • Está bueno/buena about a person means they're good-looking, not kind.
  • If you mean kind: es buena persona.

rico

  • Es rico = he has money.
  • Está rico = it tastes great.
  • Say la paella es rica and you've told me the paella is wealthy.
When you're not sure, fall back on the rule: describe the person with ser, describe the moment with estar.
