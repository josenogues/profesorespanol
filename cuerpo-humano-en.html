Doler, romperse, and talking about health

The verb doler (to hurt) works like gustar: it doesn't agree with the person, but with the body part. That's why we say me duele (singular) or me duelen (plural).

ExpressionExample
doler (me/te/le duele...)Me duele la cabeza. (My head hurts)
doler (plural)Me duelen los pies. (My feet hurt)
tener dolor de...Tengo dolor de espalda. (I have back pain)
romperseSe ha roto el brazo esquiando. (He broke his arm skiing)
torcerseMe torcí el tobillo jugando al fútbol. (I twisted my ankle)
estar resfriado/aEstoy resfriado, no puedo ir a clase. (I have a cold)
tener fiebreEl niño tiene fiebre desde ayer. (He has a fever)
encontrarse mal / bienHoy me encuentro un poco mal. (I feel a bit unwell today)
Common trap: you don't say "mi cabeza me duele" with the same word order as in English. The natural way in Spanish is me duele la cabeza, with the verb before the subject (the body part) and no possessive — the possessive is almost never used with body parts: you say "me duele la cabeza", not "me duele mi cabeza".
