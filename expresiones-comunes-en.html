Common expressions

Set phrases that native speakers use constantly in everyday conversation — they don't always translate literally.

Very common expressions

ExpressionMeaning
No hay de qué Reply to "thank you", like "you're welcome"
¡Qué va! Emphatic "no way!"
Estar en las nubes To be distracted, "have your head in the clouds"
Dar en el clavo To hit the nail on the head
Tomar el pelo To pull someone's leg / tease
Ponerse las pilas To get your act together, step it up
Costar un ojo de la cara To cost an arm and a leg
No tener pelos en la lengua To not mince words, speak bluntly
Meter la pata To put your foot in it, make a blunder
Estar como una cabra To be a bit crazy (affectionately)

Greetings and goodbyes

ExpressionMeaning
¡Qué alegría verte! Warm greeting when you run into someone
¿Qué tal todo? Informal way of asking how everything's going
¡Cuánto tiempo! "It's been ages!" — said after not seeing someone in a long time
Nos vemos Informal goodbye, "see you"
Que vaya bien Farewell wishing someone well
Un abrazo / Un beso Affectionate sign-off, typical when talking or writing to friends
Hasta la próxima "Until next time"

At the table

ExpressionMeaning
¡Que aproveche! Said before eating, "enjoy your meal"
¿Me pasas la sal? Politely asking for something at the table
Está buenísimo/a Saying the food is delicious
Estoy lleno/a Saying you can't eat any more
¿Repites? Offering or asking if someone wants seconds
¡Salud! A toast before drinking
Se me hace la boca agua Really wanting to eat something, "my mouth is watering"

At work

ExpressionMeaning
¿Cómo va todo por la oficina? Informal way to ask how work is going for someone
Se me ha hecho tarde Explaining that you ended up running late
Estoy hasta arriba de trabajo Having a ton of work, being swamped
¿Tienes un minuto? Asking a colleague for a bit of their time
Vamos a hacer una pausa Suggesting a short break
¡Buen finde! Friday sign-off wishing someone a good weekend
Nos ponemos al día Meeting up to catch up on news

With friends and at the bar

ExpressionMeaning
¿Qué te pongo? What the bartender asks when taking your order
¿Vamos a tomar algo? Suggesting going for a drink with friends
Ponme una caña Ordering a small draft beer (typical in Spain)
Te invito yo Offering to pay for someone else
¡Va por ti! A toast dedicated to someone
¿Nos tomamos otra? Suggesting having one more drink
Estamos de charla Saying you're hanging out chatting

Reacting: surprise, agreement and disagreement

ExpressionMeaning
¡No me digas! Expressing surprise at some news, "no way!"
¡Qué fuerte! Reacting to something shocking or over the top
Tienes toda la razón Showing full agreement with someone
Yo también lo creo Saying you think the same thing
No estoy de acuerdo Politely expressing disagreement
Depende Answer when something isn't a clear yes or no
¡Ni hablar! A flat refusal, "no way, absolutely not"
How to practice them: don't translate word-for-word — learn each one as a complete block tied to a situation. Some map neatly onto an English idiom (like "an arm and a leg"), but most don't, so don't assume there's always a direct match.
