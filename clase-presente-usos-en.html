The present does more than you think

You have the forms. Now the jobs: three things English says with other tenses or with shall and can, and Spanish says with the plain present.

1. «I’ve been living here for two years»

English needs have been for something that started in the past and is still going on. Spanish doesn’t: if it is still true now, it goes in the plain present.

EnglishSpanish
I’ve been living here for two years.Vivo aquí desde hace dos años.
I’ve known her since 2019.La conozco desde 2019.
I’ve been studying Spanish since January.Estudio español desde enero.
How long have you been waiting?¿Cuánto tiempo hace que esperas?
How long have you lived here?¿Desde cuándo vives aquí?
The sentence that gives you away: He vivido aquí por dos años. It’s English with Spanish words — and the por is wrong too. Still true today? Present: vivo aquí desde hace dos años.

2. desde, desde hace, hace… que

Three small pieces, and choosing between them is easy: is it a moment, or an amount of time?

piecegoes withexample
desde — sincea moment: a date, a month, a dayTrabajo aquí desde marzo.
I’ve been working here since March.
desde hace — foran amount of time you can countTrabajo aquí desde hace tres meses.
I’ve been working here for three months.
hace … que — forthe same, but said firstHace tres meses que trabajo aquí.
I’ve been working here for three months.
Can you count it? Tres meses, dos años, mucho tiempo — then you need hace. Marzo, 2019, el lunes — just desde. Later you will also hear llevo tres meses aquí: same meaning, one more way to say it.

3. «Shall I…?», «Can you…?» — just ask in the present

Offering to do something, or asking someone for a small favour: English reaches for shall or can. Spanish uses the present with a question mark, and that’s it.

EnglishSpanish
Shall I open the window?¿Abro la ventana?
Shall I help you?¿Te ayudo?
Shall I pay?¿Pago yo?
Shall we go?¿Vamos?
Can you pass me the salt?¿Me pasas la sal?
Can you lend me a pen?¿Me dejas un boli?
No «shall» to translate. ¿Abriré la ventana? sounds like you’re asking about your own future. And ¿puedes pasarme la sal? is correct, but at the table what you’ll hear is ¿me pasas la sal?

4. Telling a story before you know the past

You haven’t learnt the past tenses yet, and you can already tell a story. Spaniards tell anecdotes, jokes and film plots in the present all the time — it makes them more alive.

SpanishEnglish
Ayer salgo de casa y empieza a llover.Yesterday I leave the house and it starts to rain.
Vuelvo a buscar el paraguas…I go back to get my umbrella…
…y cuando salgo otra vez, ya no llueve.…and when I go out again, it’s stopped raining.
with casiwhat it means
Casi me caigo.I almost fell.
Casi pierdo el tren.I nearly missed the train.
Casi lo olvido.I almost forgot.
Casi + present = it nearly happened. It’s about the past, but the verb is in the present. This isn’t a trick for beginners: it’s how everybody says it.
