Por and para, once and for all

English says «for» and stops thinking. Spanish makes you choose, and the choice is not about the word — it is about which way you are pointing.

1. One picture, and you have most of it

Forget the lists for a second. Para looks forward: where you are going, what you want, who gets it. Por looks back: what caused it, what you swapped for it, what you went through.

para — forward →por — backward ←
the ideadestination, purpose, deadlinecause, exchange, route
exampleSalgo para Madrid.Paso por Madrid.
Madrid is where I end upMadrid is on the way
exampleLo hago para ti.Lo hago por ti.
you will get ityou are the reason
Never translate «for». Ask yourself the question instead: what for? → para. why? because of what? → por. That one question settles most of the cases you will meet this week.

2. para — the arrow forward

Four jobs, and they are all the same idea pointing ahead.

jobexample
where you are goingEl tren para Valencia.
what it is forUna taza para el café. · Estudio para aprender.
who gets itEste regalo es para mi madre.
the deadlineLo necesito para el viernes.
in my opinionPara mí, está bien.
Para + infinitive is «in order to». Estudio para aprender — I study to learn. If you can put «in order to» in the English, it is para and never por.

3. por — the arrow backward

More jobs, but the same idea: something that came before, or something you pass through.

jobexample
the causeLo hice por amor. · Cerrado por vacaciones.
the exchange, the priceLo compré por veinte euros. · Gracias por todo.
through, aroundPaseo por el parque. · Está por aquí.
how, the meansTe llamo por teléfono. · Lo mandé por email.
parts of the dayPor la mañana, por la tarde.
Gracias por, never gracias para. You are thanking somebody for something that already happened — it is a cause, so it is por. Same with perdón por el retraso.

4. The pairs that change the meaning

Here the two are both correct and say different things. This is where it stops being a rule and starts being a choice.

parapor
Trabajo para Juan.
he is my boss		Trabajo por Juan.
he is ill, I am covering his shift
Salgo para Madrid.
Madrid is my destination		Salgo por Madrid.
I am leaving through Madrid
Lo hago para ti.
you will get it		Lo hago por ti.
you are my reason
Es para mi madre.
she is the one who gets it		Lo hice por mi madre.
she is why I did it
«I did it for you» is two different sentences in Spanish. English hides the difference and you have to put it back. Say por ti to someone who asked you a favour, and para ti when you hand them a present.

5. The ones you just learn

A handful of set phrases where asking the question does not help. They are frequent enough to be worth memorising as single words.

Always por

  • por favor — please
  • por fin — at last
  • por supuesto — of course
  • por ejemplo — for example
  • por eso — that is why
  • por cierto — by the way

Always para

  • para siempre — forever
  • para nada — not at all
  • para colmo — to top it off
  • ¿para qué? — what for?
  • estar para — to be about to
Two questions that are not the same. ¿Por qué? is «why?» and ¿para qué? is «what for?». A Spaniard will answer them differently: porque tenía hambre versus para comer.
