The future, and what people say instead

The easiest tense in Spanish to build — and then the three ways Spaniards actually talk about tomorrow, only one of which is this tense.

1. You do not take anything off

Every other tense makes you cut the verb first: hablar → habl-. The future is the one that does not. You keep the whole infinitive and add the ending to the end of it.

hablarcomervivir
yohablarécomeréviviré
túhablaráscomerásvivirás
él / ella / ustedhablarácomerávivirá
nosotroshablaremoscomeremosviviremos
vosotroshablaréiscomeréisviviréis
ellos / ustedeshablaráncomeránvivirán
One set of endings for all three verb groups. Look at the three columns: the bold part is identical. No -ar / -er / -ir split, no separate list to learn. This is the only tense that lets you off.

2. The tildes, and the one form without

Five of the six carry a written accent. One does not, and it is the one you will get wrong.

formtilde?
hablaré · hablarás · hablaráyes
hablaremosnothe odd one out
hablaréis · hablarányes
And there is no «will». English needs two words — «I will speak». Spanish needs one: hablaré. Nothing goes in front of it.

3. Now the useful half: three ways to say tomorrow

All three exist, all three are correct, and they are not interchangeable. The one the textbook teaches first is the one you will hear least.

what it is forexample
Presentarranged, on the calendarMañana trabajo en casa.
ir a + infinitiveplans and intentions — the everyday oneMañana voy a trabajar en casa.
Future simplepredictions, promises, things out of your handsLloverá el martes. · Te lo prometo, llamaré.
For a plan, use voy a. Mañana trabajaré en casa is not wrong, but it sounds like a weather forecast about yourself. Spaniards keep the future simple for what they cannot control, and for promises.

4. The irregulars change the stem, never the endings

Twelve verbs refuse to keep the whole infinitive. The endings stay exactly the same, so learning these is learning twelve short words.

verbstemyo
tenertendr-tendré
ponerpondr-pondré
salirsaldr-saldré
venirvendr-vendré
hacerhar-haré
decirdir-diré
poderpodr-podré
sabersabr-sabré
quererquerr-querré
haberhabr-habrá (there will be)
Look at the first four: they all grow a d. tendr-, pondr-, saldr-, vendr-. And the next two just get shorter. That is the whole list — there is no second page.

5. The use nobody warns you about: guessing

This is the future you will hear most in Spain, and it is not about the future at all. It is how you say «it must be» or «probably».

they sayit means
— ¿Qué hora es? — Serán las tres.It must be about three.
¿Dónde está Ana? — Estará en casa.She is probably at home.
Tendrá unos cuarenta años.He must be about forty.
Habrá unas cien personas.There are probably about a hundred people.
Present tense, future form. Serán las tres is not «it will be three» — it is three o’clock right now, and the speaker is not sure. If you answer ¿qué hora es? with son las tres you are certain; with serán las tres you are guessing.
