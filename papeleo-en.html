Vocabulary

Papeleo y cita previa

In Spain almost nothing happens without a cita previa and a piece of paper to prove it. These are the words and phrases you'll need at any counter.

Back to all topics

Pedir cita previa

Quería pedir cita previa para el NIE.

This comes before everything else: no appointment, no service. You book online or by phone, and the wait can be weeks.

Empadronarse

Vengo a empadronarme. / Necesito el certificado de empadronamiento.

Registering on your town hall's list of residents. Nearly every other procedure depends on it, so it's usually the first one you do.

Pedir un justificante

¿Me puede dar un justificante de que lo he presentado?

The paper proving you completed the procedure. Always keep it: until the decision comes through, it's the only thing you have.

The words you'll see everywhere

WordWhat it is
la cita previaThe appointment slot you're given. Without one you can't get in.
el trámiteAny official procedure. It's the neutral word, and the one they use.
el NIEThe identity number for foreigners. You need it for almost everything.
el padrón · empadronarseA town's register of residents; empadronarse means signing on to it.
la ventanillaThe counter where you're served. «Vaya a la ventanilla 4.»
el justificanteThe paper proving you submitted or paid something.
la tasaThe fee for the procedure. Usually paid at a bank beforehand.
el expedienteYour case, with its file number. «Su expediente está en trámite.»
el funcionario / la funcionariaThe civil servant who deals with you.
Papeleo or trámite: «papeleo» is colloquial and carries a groan («vaya papeleo»). «Trámite» is the neutral word and the one you'll see written down. And «los papeles» means the documents: «¿traes todos los papeles?».

Filling in a form

The fields are always the same, and one of them catches everyone out.

FieldWhat they want
NombreFirst name only.
ApellidosSpain uses two: the father's and the mother's. If you only have one, the second is left blank.
DomicilioYour address: street, number, floor and door.
Código postalThe five digits for your area.
Fecha de nacimientoIn day/month/year order.
DNI / NIEYour ID document, or the NIE if you're a foreigner.
FirmaYour handwritten signature. On paper, a digital signature almost never counts.

Rellenar y firmar

Rellene el formulario · Firme aquí

Rellenar is filling in the details; firmar is signing. Don't say «llenar el formulario».

Adjuntar

Tiene que adjuntar una copia del pasaporte

Attaching a document to the application. At a counter they'll say «adjunte una copia».

Presentar

Presente la solicitud antes del viernes

Submitting officially. «Presentar la solicitud» isn't the same as just posting it.

At the counter

SituationWhat you say
You arrive for your appointmentTengo cita a las diez a nombre de Rossi.
You're not sure it's the right place¿Es aquí donde se hace el empadronamiento?
You're collecting something that's readyVengo a recoger la tarjeta sanitaria.
They tell you a document is missing¿Qué me falta exactamente?
You want to know how long it takes¿Cuánto tardan en resolverlo?
You want proof of what you handed in¿Me puede dar un justificante?
Before you go, always check which documents to bring and whether there's a fee to pay. If one paper is missing they send you home and you have to book another appointment — which can be weeks later. It's the mistake that most often wastes a trip.
