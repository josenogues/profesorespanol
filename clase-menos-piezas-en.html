Drop the «yo», drop the «estoy»

The two habits that make a sentence sound translated

1. The subject is already in the verb

English can't drop it: work on its own doesn't say who. Spanish puts the person in the ending, so the pronoun is already there. Saying it every time sounds like a robot — or like you're arguing with someone.

the ending already says it
trabajoyo
trabajastú
trabajaél / ella / usted
trabajamosnosotros
trabajáisvosotros
trabajanellos / ellas

I work in MadridTrabajo en MadridYo trabajo en Madrid
Do you have a car?¿Tienes coche?¿Tú tienes coche?
She lives hereVive aquíElla vive aquí

2. When you do say it

Three cases, and they're all the same idea: when the person is the point.

Contrast

  • Yo voy, tú te quedas.
  • Ella sí, él no.

To clear up who

  • trabaja is he, she or you (usted).
  • Él trabaja aquí.

On its own

  • — ¿Quién ha sido? — Yo.
  • — Yo también.
The test that always works: if you'd stress the word in English — «I work in Madrid, not him» — then say it in Spanish. If you wouldn't, leave it out.

3. The «estoy» that English needs and Spanish doesn't

English uses the -ing form for nearly everything going on: I'm working, I'm living in Madrid, I'm having lunch with Ana on Friday. Spanish uses the plain present far more often.

EnglishSpanishrarely or never
I'm living in MadridVivo en MadridEstoy viviendo en Madrid
What are you doing?¿Qué haces?¿Qué estás haciendo? only if you mean this very second
I'm working tomorrowMañana trabajoEstoy trabajando mañana
I'm having lunch with Ana on FridayEl viernes como con Ana

estar + gerundio is right when you're in the middle of it and you could add ahora mismo: — ¿Puedes hablar? — Ahora no, estoy comiendo.

And one rule with no exceptions: it never means the future. English says I'm going tomorrow; Spanish says mañana voy, never estoy yendo mañana.

4. Two more pieces Spanish throws away

No «my» with your own body

  • Me duele la cabeza. mi cabeza
  • Me pongo el abrigo. mi abrigo
  • If it's yours, the pronoun already said so.

No «it» as a subject

  • Llueve. — it's raining
  • Es tarde. — it's late
  • Hace frío. — it's cold
  • There is no word for «it» here at all.
The giveaway sentence, before and after: Yo estoy trabajando en un banco → Trabajo en un banco. Three words shorter, and nobody can tell you're not from here.
