The five compound tenses

Compound tenses in Spanish are formed using an auxiliary verb and a past participle. The auxiliary verb is always haber (to have), conjugated in the appropriate simple tense. The past participle remains invariable.

To form a compound tense, you take the conjugated form of haber from the table above and add the past participle of the main verb. For example, he hablado (I have spoken), había comido (I had eaten), habrá vivido (he will have lived).

The past participle of regular verbs is formed by dropping the infinitive ending (-AR, -ER, -IR) and adding -ADO for -AR verbs, or -IDO for -ER and -IR verbs (e.g., hablar → hablado, comer → comido, vivir → vivido).

Tener — to have (possession)

Don't confuse haber (auxiliary "to have") with tener (possession, obligation with "tener que"). Full conjugation:
