Saying it without sounding like an order

Three tools you already know — conditional, imperfect and subjunctive — do all the work.

1. Why it sounds blunt

Business Spanish softens a lot, above all when asking and when disagreeing. A perfectly correct sentence can sound like an order purely because it is in the present and the imperative. It isn't about vocabulary: it is about tense.

2. The three tools

You already know all three. What's new is what they're for here.

The conditional

  • Turns an order into a request.
  • ¿Podría mandarme el albarán?
  • Sería mejor cambiar de fecha.

The courtesy imperfect

  • Pushes the request back in time and makes it less head-on.
  • Quería hablar con el responsable.
  • Venía a preguntar por el pedido.

The subjunctive

  • Presents your idea as a possibility, not a fact.
  • Le agradecería que me respondiera hoy.
  • No creo que sea la mejor opción.

4. The «no» that doesn't say no

A flat no is rare in a negotiation. The usual move is to say it's difficult and leave the door open:

Instead of «no»

  • Lo veo complicado.
  • Nos resultaría difícil.
  • Habría que verlo con calma.

And if you do want to shut it

  • Eso no es viable.
  • No estoy de acuerdo en absoluto.
  • That one really is a no: use it when you mean it.
If you ask for something in the imperative and you aren't the boss, it sounds like an order. With a conditional in front, it's a request. It is the change that pays off most in an email.
