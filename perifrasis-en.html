Perífrasis verbales

Two verbs working together: one carries the person and the tense, the other says what happens.

How they work

A perífrasis is two verbs in a row that mean one thing. The first one is conjugated and is the one in charge: it says who and when. The second one is an infinitive, a gerund or a participle, and says what happens.

In tengo que estudiar, tengo says it's me and it's now; estudiar says what has to be done. Change the first one and everything moves: tenía que estudiar, tendré que estudiar.

Obligation and need

PeriphrasisWhat it saysExample
tener que + infinitivoYour own, concrete obligation. By far the most used one.Tengo que llamar al banco.
hay que + infinitivoA general obligation, with no particular person. It never changes form.Hay que reservar antes.
deber + infinitivoA moral duty, more serious. It sounds like advice, or a reproach.Debes decírselo.
deber de + infinitivoThis is not an obligation: it is a guess. «I suppose he's asleep».Debe de estar durmiendo.
necesitar + infinitivoA need, not a duty. Nobody is imposing it on you.Necesito dormir.

Future and intention

PeriphrasisWhat it saysExample
ir a + infinitivoNear future or intention. In speech it is used far more than the simple future.Voy a llamarte luego.
estar a punto de + infinitivoAbout to happen, any second now.Está a punto de salir.
pensar + infinitivoA plan you already have in mind.Pienso mudarme en junio.

Duration: what is going on

PeriphrasisWhat it saysExample
estar + gerundioGoing on right now. If «ahora mismo» doesn't fit, you probably don't need it.Estoy cocinando.
seguir / continuar + gerundioIt's still going on, it hasn't stopped.Sigo viviendo aquí.
llevar + gerundioHow long you have been at it. Careful: not a perfect tense — llevar.Llevo dos años estudiando español.
ir + gerundioLittle by little, gradually.Voy entendiendo.
andar + gerundioColloquial: going around doing it, without finishing.Anda buscando piso.

Beginning and end

PeriphrasisWhat it saysExample
empezar / comenzar a + infinitivoThe beginning, plain and simple.Empecé a trabajar en marzo.
ponerse a + infinitivoTo start suddenly, or with gusto.Se puso a llover.
echarse a + infinitivoSuddenly and helplessly. It goes with reír, llorar, correr, temblar.Se echó a reír.
dejar de + infinitivoTo stop. Careful: no dejo de pensar en ello = I can't stop thinking about it.He dejado de fumar.
acabar / terminar de + infinitivoJust finished, a moment ago. Also the plain ending: termina de comer.Acabo de llegar.
llegar a + infinitivoTo go as far as, more than you'd expect.Llegó a ganar tres premios.

Repetition and habit

PeriphrasisWhat it saysExample
volver a + infinitivoAgain. Spanish says this with the verb, not by adding «otra vez».He vuelto a llamar.
soler + infinitivoWhat you usually do. It only exists in the present and the imperfect.Suelo desayunar a las ocho.

The three that get mixed up most

deber or deber de

Debes ir = you must go. Debe de ser tarde = I reckon it's late. One letter, two different things.

acabar de or acabar + gerund

Acabo de comer = I ate a moment ago. Acabé comiendo solo = I ended up eating on my own.

volver a, which almost nobody uses

Spanish doesn't say comí otra vez, it says volví a comer. Once you start saying it that way, it is much harder to tell where you're from.
