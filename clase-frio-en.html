Cold in three ways

The weather does it, you have it, the thing is it

The previous class: «One «to be» in English, three in Spanish»

1. Three colds, three verbs

English uses to be for all of them: it's cold, I'm cold, the coffee's cold. Spanish uses a different verb depending on who is cold.

EnglishSpanishwho's cold
It's cold todayHoy hace fríothe weather
I'm coldTengo fríome
The coffee is coldEl café está fríothe coffee

Same with heat, with one trap: hace calor and tengo calor, but the thing is está caliente, never está calor. After hace and tengo you put a noun; after está, an adjective.

2. What you have in Spanish

All of these are to be in English and tener in Spanish. Learn them as a block — they come up every single day.

EnglishSpanish
I'm hungry / thirstyTengo hambre / sed
I'm sleepyTengo sueño
I'm scaredTengo miedo
I'm in a hurryTengo prisa
You're rightTienes razón
I'm luckyTengo suerte
I'm 30Tengo 30 años
The one everybody gets wrong on day one: Soy 30 años → Tengo 30 años. And the question is ¿Cuántos años tienes? — how many years do you have.

3. The weather does itself

With hace + noun

  • Hace frío / calor
  • Hace sol
  • Hace viento
  • Hace buen / mal tiempo

On their own

  • Llueve — it rains
  • Nieva — it snows
  • Está nublado — it's cloudy
  • Hay niebla — it's foggy

Notice the last one: fog exists, so it takes hay, like in the previous class.

Homework

Tell me in writing what the weather was like this week and how you were: use hace, tengo and está at least twice each.
