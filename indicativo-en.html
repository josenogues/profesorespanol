Indicative Mood

The indicative mood expresses facts and real or likely actions. It has 5 simple tenses and 5 compound tenses — here are full conjugations for all ten, using hablar/comer/vivir as examples.

Simple tensesCompound tensesWhen it's used
Presente (canto / I sing)Pretérito perfecto compuesto (he cantado / I have sung)Present actions, habits, general truths / Recent actions with a present-day effect
Pretérito perfecto simple (canté / I sang)Pretérito anterior (hube cantado / I had sung)Completed past actions, narrative / Past actions immediately preceding other past actions (rare)
Pretérito imperfecto (cantaba / I used to sing)Pretérito pluscuamperfecto (había cantado / I had sung)Descriptions in the past, habitual past actions, narrative context / Past actions prior to other past actions
Futuro simple (cantaré / I will sing)Futuro compuesto (habré cantado / I will have sung)Future actions, promises, present-day probability / Future actions prior to other future actions
Condicional simple (cantaría / I would sing)Condicional compuesto (habría cantado / I would have sung)Hypotheses, wishes, politeness, conditioned actions / Past conditioned actions, past hypotheses
Compound tenses are always formed with haber conjugated in the matching simple tense + the main verb's participle (unchanging: hablado, comido, vivido).
