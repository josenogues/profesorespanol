The little «a», and lo, la, le

Two things English simply does not have: a preposition in front of people, and a pronoun that goes before the verb instead of after it. Until you have these, half of what you hear sounds scrambled.

1. People take an «a». Things do not.

When the thing receiving the action is a person, Spanish puts an a in front of it. It translates to nothing at all in English.

a thing — no aa person — with a
Veo la casa.Veo a María.
Busco mi móvil.Busco a mi hermano.
Conozco Madrid.Conozco a tu madre.
Llevo el coche al taller.Llevo a los niños al colegio.
Pets get one too. Busco a mi perro — because he is somebody to you. A stray dog you have never met is busco un perro. It is about whether the thing is a someone, not about grammar gender.

2. Why it is not decoration

In English the order of the words tells you who does what. In Spanish the order moves around, so the a is what tells you.

Spanishwho is doing it
Juan busca a María.Juan is looking
A Juan lo busca María.María is looking
¿A quién buscas?Who are you looking for?
¿Quién te busca?Who is looking for you?
Drop the a and the sentence changes owner. That is why Spaniards can put the object first and still be understood — and why leaving it out does not sound like a small mistake to them, it sounds like you said something else.

3. lo, la, los, las — and they go in front

Once you have named the thing, you replace it. English puts «it» after the verb; Spanish puts it before.

you askedyou answerEnglish
¿Ves la casa?Sí, la veo.Yes, I see it.
¿Tienes el libro?Sí, lo tengo.Yes, I have it.
¿Conoces a mis padres?Sí, los conozco.Yes, I know them.
¿Compraste las entradas?Sí, las compré.Yes, I bought them.
Before the verb, always — except in three places. With an infinitive, a gerund or an order, it sticks to the end instead: voy a verlo, estoy viéndolo, cómpralas. Everywhere else it goes in front.

4. le and les — the one who receives

Different pronoun for the person you give something to. English says «to him»; Spanish says le, and says it even when the name is right there.

SpanishEnglish
Le doy el libro a María.I give the book to María.
Le digo la verdad.I tell him / her the truth.
Les escribo a mis padres.I write to my parents.
¿Le has preguntado?Have you asked him / her?
Yes, it is doubled on purpose. Le doy el libro a María says «her» twice, and that is the normal sentence — doy el libro a María sounds incomplete to a Spanish ear. You already met this le in the gustar class: le gusta.

5. Both at once: the famous «se lo»

When the two pronouns meet, le turns into se. There is no reason for it other than that le lo is unpronounceable.

instead ofyou say
le lo doyse lo doyI give it to him
le la digose la digoI tell it to her
les las mandose las mandoI send them to them
The order never changes: person first, thing second. Se (who) + lo (what). English does it the other way round — «give it to him» — so this is one to say out loud a few times before it sticks.
