Things please you

In Spanish you don’t like things — things please you. That is gustar, and the whole family that works the same way.

1. One sentence, back to front

Me gusta el café does not mean «I like the coffee». It means «the coffee pleases me». The coffee is doing the verb, not you.

who does the verbsentence
EnglishII like coffee
Spanishel caféMe gusta el café

That is the whole class. Everything below is a consequence of it — including the mistakes.

So «I like coffee» is never Yo gusto el café. Yo gusto means «I am pleasing» — that you are the nice thing other people enjoy. It is a real sentence, just not the one you wanted.

2. gusta or gustan? Look at the thing, not the person

One thing → gusta. More than one → gustan. It never changes with who likes it.

one thingseveral things
to meMe gusta el caféMe gustan los perros
to youTe gustaTe gustan
to him / her / you (usted)Le gustaLe gustan
to usNos gustaNos gustan
to you (plural)Os gustaOs gustan
to themLes gustaLes gustan

With a verb it is always singular, even with two of them: Me gusta bailar. Me gusta leer y escribir.

3. How much, from the top to the bottom

The ladder you will use every day. Notice that the bottom one needs nada — «not at all».

Me encanta el café.I love it
Me gusta mucho el café.I like it a lot
Me gusta el café.I like it
No me gusta mucho el café.I don’t like it much
No me gusta nada el café.I don’t like it at all
No me encanta mucho. Encantar is already the top of the ladder, so it takes no «a lot». Same in English: you don’t say «I love it a lot» to make it stronger.

Practice 1 — the shape

Four quick ones. The point is always the same: look at the thing.

Choose Done

Chocolate pleases me. → ___ gusta el chocolate.

Choose Done

Me ___ los perros. (dogs — more than one)

Write it Done

Reading pleases me. → Me ___ leer. (gusta or gustan?)

Choose Done

Marta likes the cinema. → A Marta ___ gusta el cine.

4. The same shape, nine more verbs

Once you have gustar, you have all of these for free. Same slots, same rule about the thing.

Every day

  • encantar — to love. Me encanta Madrid.
  • doler — to hurt. Me duele la cabeza.
  • apetecer — to fancy. ¿Te apetece un café?

Nearly every day

  • interesar — No me interesa.
  • importar — to mind. No me importa.
  • faltar — to be missing. Me faltan dos euros.
  • quedar — to have left / to suit. Me quedan tres días. Te queda bien.
  • parecer — to seem. Me parece bien.
  • molestar — to bother. ¿Te molesta la música?
Me duele la cabeza, not mi cabeza duele. The head is doing the hurting, to you. And it is la cabeza, not mi cabeza — the me already said whose head it is. Spanish almost never puts «my» on a body part.

5. All of it, in one conversation

Read it out loud with me, one part each, then swap. Six of today’s verbs are in here — see if you can spot them before you look.

— ¿Te apetece un café?

— La verdad, no me gusta mucho el café. ¿Hay té?

— Sí. A mí me encanta el té verde.

— A mí también. Oye, ¿qué te parece este sitio?

— Me parece muy bonito. Pero me duelen los pies.

— A mí también. ¿Nos sentamos?

— Vale. ¿Te importa si pido algo de comer?

— No, claro que no. A mí me faltan dos euros, ¿me los dejas?

The six: apetecer, gustar, encantar, parecer, doler, importar — and faltar makes seven. Not one of them has a «yo» anywhere.

6. The «a mí» that looks like a mistake

A mí me gusta is not saying it twice — the a mí is there for emphasis and you can drop it. But as soon as you name the person, you need both pieces:

Me gusta el fútbol.neutral
A mí me gusta el fútbol.…whatever you think
A Peter le gusta el fútbol.name it → a + keep the le

There is a reason for it: le can mean him, her or usted. A Peter says which one.

Careful with people: me gustas means you fancy them. If you just get on well with someone, it is me cae bien. Mi jefe me gusta and mi jefe me cae bien are two very different conversations.

7. «Me too» and «me neither»

This is where the back-to-front sentence pays off. You cannot answer yo también, because you are not the subject — you live in the a mí slot. So the answer starts there too.

they sayyou agreeyou disagree
Me gusta el cine.A mí también.A mí no.
No me gusta el cine.A mí tampoco.A mí sí.

Four little answers, and you can hold a whole conversation about what you both like. Change the person and they still work: a él también, a nosotros tampoco, a ellos sí.

Practice 3 — answering back

Read what they say, then answer. Two agree, two disagree.

Choose Done

— Me gusta el cine. — (you like it too) A mí ___.

Choose Done

— No me gusta el fútbol. — (you don’t either) A mí ___.

Choose Done

— No me gusta el café. — (but you do like it) A mí ___.

Write it Done

Say it in Spanish: I love Madrid.

8. Now you talk

The last stretch of the class, out loud and without writing anything down. No right answers here — the only rule is that no sentence may start with yo.

  1. Ask me three ¿te gusta…? questions. One about food, one about a city, one about a film.
  2. I will tell you five things about me. React to every one with a mí también / a mí tampoco / a mí sí / a mí no — and nothing else.
  3. Tell me two things that hurt right now, real or invented. Me duele… / Me duelen…
  4. Tell me what you fancy doing this weekend: Me apetece… And one thing that does not interest you at all: No me interesa nada…
  5. Last one, harder: say something about someone else. A mi hermano le…, a mis padres les…

Homework

Write me ten sentences: three things you love, three you don’t like at all, two about someone else (a mi hermano le…), one thing that hurts, and one ¿te apetece…? question for me.
