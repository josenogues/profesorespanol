Things please you

In Spanish you don’t like things — things please you. That is gustar, and the whole family that works the same way

1. The sentence is the other way round

Me gusta el café does not mean «I like the coffee». It means «the coffee pleases me». The coffee is doing the verb, not you.

who does the verbsentence
EnglishII like coffee
Spanishel caféMe gusta el café

That's the whole class. Everything below is a consequence of it.

2. gusta or gustan? Look at the thing, not the person

One thing → gusta. More than one → gustan. It never changes with who likes it.

one thingseveral things
to meMe gusta el caféMe gustan los perros
to youTe gustaTe gustan
to him / her / you (usted)Le gustaLe gustan
to usNos gustaNos gustan
to you (plural)Os gustaOs gustan
to themLes gustaLes gustan

With a verb it's always singular, even two of them: Me gusta bailar. Me gusta leer y escribir.

3. The same shape, six more verbs

Once you've got gustar, you've got all of these for free.

Every day

  • encantar — love it. Me encanta Madrid.
  • doler — hurt. Me duele la cabeza.
  • apetecer — fancy. ¿Te apetece un café?

Nearly every day

  • faltar — be missing. Me faltan dos euros.
  • interesar. No me interesa.
  • quedar — suit. Te queda bien.

Note me duele la cabeza: in Spanish the head hurts you. You never «have» a headache.

4. The «a mí» that looks like a mistake

A mí me gusta isn't saying it twice — the a mí is there for emphasis and you can drop it. But as soon as you name the person, you need both pieces:

Me gusta el fútbol.neutral
A mí me gusta el fútbol.…whatever you think
A Peter le gusta el fútbol.name it → a + keep the le
Careful with people: me gustas means you fancy them. If you just get on well with someone, it's me cae bien.

Homework

Send me five things you like, five you don't, and one that hurts. Use gustar, encantar and doler.
