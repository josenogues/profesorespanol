Business Spanish

Registro y tono (register and tone)

Workplace Spanish has no grammar of its own. What changes is tone: how much you wrap around what you're asking for. Overdoing it and being too blunt are equally visible mistakes.

¿Tú or usted?

SituationWhat to use
Colleagues at your company (Spain)Tú, almost always, even with bosses
New client, first contactUsted until they switch
Latin America generallyUsted more often than in Spain
Banks, lawyers, governmentUsted
Young companies, startupsTú from minute one
English has no equivalent choice — "you" covers everything. This is a decision you have to make consciously in Spanish, every time you open your mouth. Start with usted with strangers and switch the moment they use tú with you.

How to soften a request

This is 80% of professional tone. Three tools, and they combine.

1. The conditional

  • Turns an order into a proposal
  • ¿Puedes revisarlo? → ¿Podrías revisarlo?
  • Quiero saber → Querría saber

2. The question

  • Ask as a question, not a statement
  • Necesito el informe → ¿Me podrías pasar el informe?
  • Te llamo a las 5 → ¿Te viene bien que te llame a las 5?

3. The cushion

  • A tag that takes the pressure off
  • cuando puedas → when you can
  • si no es mucha molestia → if it's no trouble
  • cuando tengas un momento

The real risk for an English speaker isn't sounding stiff — it's sounding cold. Spanish emails expect more padding than English ones: a greeting line, a closing line. Dropping straight into the request reads as brusque even when you didn't mean it.
