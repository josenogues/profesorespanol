Numbers, and what you use them for

You need them on day one — your age, your phone, a price, a date — and they have four or five places where English pushes you the wrong way.

1. From 0 to 29: one word each

Up to fifteen you just learn them. From sixteen to twenty-nine they are built — but written as one word.

Spanishhow it is built
0–5cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco
6–10seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez
11–15once, doce, trece, catorce, quincethese five end in -ce
16–19dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinuevedieci + the number
20veinte
21–29veintiuno, veintidós, veintitrés… veintinueveveinti + the number
Four of them carry an accent: dieciséis, veintidós, veintitrés and veintiséis. Alone, dos, tres and seis have none; glued to another word, they need it.

2. From 31 to 99: three words, with y in the middle

From thirty on, Spanish stops gluing. It is the ten, y, and the unit — where English puts a hyphen.

the tensand with a unit
30treinta31 · treinta y uno
40cuarenta42 · cuarenta y dos
50cincuenta55 · cincuenta y cinco
60sesenta67 · sesenta y siete
70setenta78 · setenta y ocho
80ochenta84 · ochenta y cuatro
90noventa99 · noventa y nueve
Sesenta and setenta are one letter apart, and on the phone they sound almost the same. Hook them to their number: seis → sesenta, siete → setenta.

3. Cien, ciento, mil and un millón

Here English misleads you three times: with the «and», with the «a» in a thousand, and with the dot and the comma.

Spanishwatch out
100cienexactly a hundred
101ciento unono y: not ciento y uno
150ciento cincuenta
200 · 300doscientos · trescientosone word
500 · 700 · 900quinientos · setecientos · novecientosthe three irregular ones
1.000milnot un mil
2.000dos milmil never takes an -s here
1.000.000un millón de euroswith de before the noun
1.000.000.000mil millonesa billion. Un billón is a million millions
The y lives in one place only: between the tens and the units. Ciento uno, doscientos cinco, mil diez — and then ciento treinta y uno. «One hundred and one» has no y in Spanish.
The dot and the comma are swapped. Spain writes 1.000 for a thousand and 3,5 for three and a half. A price of 12,50 € is twelve euros fifty, not twelve thousand.

4. Some numbers change with the noun

Most numbers never change. Two families do: the ones that end in uno, and the hundreds.

masculinefeminine
1un eurouna persona
21veintiún añosveintiuna personas
31treinta y un díastreinta y una horas
200doscientos eurosdoscientas personas
500quinientos metrosquinientas páginas
Uno loses its -o before a noun. Counting, it is uno, dos, tres; with a noun after it, un euro, veintiún años. English has nothing like this, so it is the one to say out loud until it stops sounding odd.

5. What you will use them for this week

Five everyday uses, each with its own small trap.

Spanishnot like English
your ageTengo treinta y dos añosyou have years, you are not them
your phoneseis, uno, dos · cuarenta y cinco · sesenta y sieteone by one, or in pairs
a price¿Cuánto cuesta? — Doce con cincuenta12,50 €: con, not «point»
a dateel tres de mayo de 2026the day first, a plain number, and two de
a yearmil novecientos noventathe whole number: never «nineteen ninety»
Dates use the plain number, not «3rd». El tres de mayo, el veinte de junio. Only the first of the month can also be el primero: el uno de mayo or el primero de mayo, both are fine.
