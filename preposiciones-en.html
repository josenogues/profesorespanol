Preposiciones

Prepositions link words together, and they're one of the hardest parts to master because their use doesn't always translate literally from English.

PrepositionMain useExample
a essentialdirection, time, direct object when it's a personVoy a Madrid (I go to Madrid). A las tres (at three). Vi a Juan (I saw Juan).
de essentialorigin, possession, materialSoy de Italia (I'm from Italy). El libro de Ana (Ana's book). Mesa de madera (wooden table).
en essentiallocation, mode of transport, time (month/year)Vivo en Roma (I live in Rome). Voy en tren (I go by train). En verano (in summer).
con essentialcompany, instrumentVoy con mi hermana (I go with my sister). Escribo con bolígrafo (I write with a pen).
por / para essentialcause/means · purpose/recipientSee the dedicated page in "Watch out for this".
sinwithoutCafé sin azúcar (coffee without sugar).
sobreon top of something, or "about"El libro está sobre la mesa (on the table). Hablamos sobre el examen (we talk about the exam).
bajounder (literal or figurative)El gato está bajo la mesa (under the table). Bajo presión (under pressure).
entrebetween/amongEntre la silla y la mesa (between the chair and the table).
desdestarting point (time or place)Desde las nueve (since nine). Desde Madrid (from Madrid).
hastaending point (time or place)Hasta las cinco (until five). Hasta Barcelona (as far as Barcelona).
haciatoward, approximate directionCaminamos hacia el centro (we walk toward downtown).
duranteduration of an actionDurante el verano trabajo mucho (during summer I work a lot).
segúnsource of informationSegún el profesor, es correcto (according to the teacher).
contraopposition, contactLuchar contra el tiempo (fight against time). Apoyado contra la pared (leaning against the wall).
anteformal presence before someoneAnte el juez (before the judge). Ante esta situación (faced with this situation).
trasafter, behind (more formal than "después de")Tras el examen, descansamos (after the exam, we rest).
Tip: focus first on a, de, en, con, por, para — they're by far the most frequent. The rest sink in naturally through exposure; no need to memorize the whole list upfront.

The personal "a"

One of the most common traps for English speakers: when the direct object of a verb is a person (or a personified animal/thing), you add "a" before it — something English never does.

With a person

Veo a María. / Busco a mi perro.

The "a" is required, even though English doesn't translate it ("I see Maria", not "I see to Maria").

Without a person

Veo la película. / Busco las llaves.

No "a" with objects.

Por / para have their own dedicated page inside "Spanish for English speakers" — they're some of the most confusing prepositions of all, so they earned separate treatment.
