Subjuntivo

The subjunctive doesn't state objective facts — it expresses wishes, doubt, emotion, or hypothetical situations. English barely has an equivalent mood anymore (a few fossilized forms like "if I were you"), so this is usually the single biggest adjustment for English speakers.

The most common triggers

Wishes

Quiero que vengas. Espero que te guste.

Verbs of wanting/hoping + "que" + subjunctive, when the subject changes.

Doubt / denial

No creo que sea verdad. Dudo que venga.

With "creer" and "pensar" in the affirmative, you use the indicative instead: Creo que es verdad.

Emotion

Me alegra que estés aquí. Es una pena que no puedas venir.

Any emotional reaction to a fact.

Impersonal expressions

Es importante que estudies. Es necesario que llegues a tiempo.

"Es + adjective + que" almost always calls for the subjunctive.

Requests and commands

Te pido que me ayudes. Dile que venga.

When you ask someone for something through a verb (pedir, decir, exigir).

Purpose/condition conjunctions

Para que entiendas. Aunque llueva, saldré. Cuando llegues, avísame (future).

"Para que", "aunque" (if hypothetical), and "cuando" referring to the future.

Indicative (real/certain fact)Subjunctive (wish/doubt/hypothesis)
Sé que viene.Espero que venga.
Creo que tiene razón.No creo que tenga razón.
Cuando llega, cenamos. (habit)Cuando llegue, cenaremos. (future)
⚠️ The classic English-speaker mistake: in English you'd say "When he arrives, we'll have dinner" — using the present tense after "when" for a future event. Spanish uses the subjunctive there instead: Cuando llegue, never "cuando llegará". This exact pattern trips up almost every English speaker at some point.
