Reflexive verbs

Reflexive verbs describe an action the subject does to themselves. They always come with a reflexive pronoun (me, te, se, nos, os, se) that matches the subject — English usually just drops this ("I shower", not "I shower myself").

Main example: Ducharse (to take a shower)

PronounVerbMeaning
Yome duchoI shower
Túte duchasyou shower
Él / Ellase duchahe/she showers
Nosotros / -asnos duchamoswe shower
Vosotros / -asos ducháisyou all shower
Ellos / Ellasse duchanthey shower

Other common reflexive verbs (present tense)

Levantarse (to get up)

me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, os levantáis, se levantan

Lavarse (to wash oneself)

me lavo, te lavas, se lava, nos lavamos, os laváis, se lavan

Llamarse (to be called)

me llamo, te llamas, se llama, nos llamamos, os llamáis, se llaman

Acostarse (o→ue) (to go to bed)

me acuesto, te acuestas, se acuesta, nos acostamos, os acostáis, se acuestan

Vestirse (e→i) (to get dressed)

me visto, te vistes, se viste, nos vestimos, os vestís, se visten

Despertarse (e→ie) (to wake up)

me despierto, te despiertas, se despierta, nos despertamos, os despertáis, se despiertan

Tip: several reflexive verbs are also stem-changing (acostarse, vestirse, despertarse) — the irregular pattern is exactly the same as the non-reflexive version, you're just adding the reflexive pronoun in front.
