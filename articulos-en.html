Grammar

Artículos

Spanish has definite articles (el, la, los, las) and indefinite articles (un, una, unos, unas). They always agree in gender and number with the noun.

Masculine singularFeminine singularMasculine pluralFeminine plural
Definiteel librola mesalos libroslas mesas
Indefiniteun librouna mesaunos librosunas mesas

Contractions

"El" always contracts with the prepositions a and de:

a + el → al

Voy al cine.

Never "a el cine".

de + el → del

Vengo del trabajo.

Never "de el trabajo".

When the article is dropped

Professions

Soy profesor. (not "soy un profesor")

Dropped unless the noun is qualified by an adjective: Soy un profesor exigente.

Proper names

María vive en Madrid.

No article with people's names or with most cities and countries.

Days of the week (with "ser")

Hoy es lunes.

But it's used to say when something happens: El lunes tengo clase.

⚠️ Heads up: English has no grammatical gender at all, so picking "el" vs "la" is usually the hardest part at the start — there's no real shortcut, just exposure. Always learn a noun together with its article (say "la mano", not just "mano") rather than memorizing them separately.
