Grammar

Artículos

Spanish has definite articles (el, la, los, las) and indefinite articles (un, una, unos, unas). They always agree in gender and number with the noun.

Masculine singularFeminine singularMasculine pluralFeminine plural
Definiteel libro (the book)la mesa (the table)los libros (the books)las mesas (the tables)
Indefiniteun libro (a book)una mesa (a table)unos libros (some books)unas mesas (some tables)

Contractions

"El" always contracts with the prepositions a and de:

a + el → al

Voy al cine.

Never "a el cine".

de + el → del

Vengo del trabajo.

Never "de el trabajo".

Watch out, these do NOT contract: "de + ella" → de ella (never "della"), "de + él" → de él (it keeps the accent mark precisely so it isn't confused with the article "el"). Only "a + el" and "de + el" contract — with any other pronoun or proper noun, never.

When the article is dropped

Professions

Soy profesor. (not "soy un profesor")

Dropped unless the noun is qualified by an adjective: Soy un profesor exigente.

Proper names

María vive en Madrid.

No article with people's names or with most cities and countries.

Days of the week (with "ser")

Hoy es lunes.

But it's used to say when something happens: El lunes tengo clase.

Heads up: English has no grammatical gender at all, so picking "el" vs "la" is usually the hardest part at the start — there's no real shortcut, just exposure. Always learn a noun together with its article (say "la mano", not just "mano") rather than memorizing them separately.
Curious exception — football team names: in Peninsular Spanish it's common to use the article with these proper names: Hoy juega el Real Madrid (Real Madrid is playing today), el Barça ganó ayer (Barça won yesterday). In other Spanish-speaking regions, like Uruguay, it's said without the article: Hoy juega Peñarol.
