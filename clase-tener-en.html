What you have in Spanish

A dozen everyday things that English is and Spanish has. They come up on your first day and they never stop.

1. The list

Learn it as one block. The pattern is always the same: tener + a noun, with no «a» and no «the» in front of it.

EnglishSpanishword for word
I’m hungryTengo hambreI have hunger
I’m thirstyTengo sedI have thirst
I’m sleepyTengo sueñoI have sleep
I’m cold / hotTengo frío / calorI have cold / heat
I’m scaredTengo miedoI have fear
I’m in a hurryTengo prisaI have hurry
You’re rightTienes razónyou have reason
I’m luckyTengo suerteI have luck
I feel like a coffeeTengo ganas de un caféI have desire of a coffee
Never estar with these. Estoy hambre, soy hambre — neither exists. And one of them is a proper banana skin: estoy caliente does not mean «I’m hot», it means something else entirely. The weather is hot with hace calor; you are hot with tengo calor.

2. «Very» is mucho here, not muy

This is the mistake that gives you away, and it has a reason: after tener comes a noun, and nouns take mucho. Muy only goes with adjectives.

you want to saySpanishnot
I’m very hungrymucha hambremuy hambre
I’m very sleepymucho sueñomuy sueño
I’m very scaredmucho miedo
I’m in a big hurrymucha prisa
I’m very tiredestoy muy cansadohere it is muy: cansado is an adjective
And it changes with the word: mucho or mucha. Mucha hambre, sed, prisa, suerte, razón. Mucho frío, calor, sueño, miedo. If you are not sure, say the plain version: tengo hambre is always right.

3. Your age is something you have

The one everybody gets wrong on day one, and you will be asked it in your first conversation.

EnglishSpanish
How old are you?¿Cuántos años tienes?
I’m thirtyTengo treinta años
My son is fiveMi hijo tiene cinco años
I turn thirty in MayCumplo treinta en mayo
The años is not optional. Tengo treinta on its own means nothing — thirty what? And the question is literally «how many years do you have», so ¿Cómo viejo eres? is not a thing.

4. Tener ganas de and tener que

Two that take a verb after them instead of a noun. They are the two you will use most once the list above is automatic.

SpanishEnglishwhat follows
Tengo ganas de salirI feel like going outthe infinitive
No tengo ganas de nadaI don’t feel like anythingor a noun
Tengo que trabajarI have to workthe infinitive
Careful with the little word in the middle. Ganas de salir but tengo que salir — different word, different meaning. There is a whole class on tener que and its friends when you want it.
