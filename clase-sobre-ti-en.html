Tell me about you

Five questions you will be asked for the rest of your life in Spanish, and the four verbs that answer all of them.

1. The five questions

Learn the answers and you can hold your first conversation today. Nobody will ask you anything else in the first five minutes.

they askyou answer
¿Cómo te llamas?Me llamo Brandon.
¿De dónde eres?Soy de Londres. · Soy inglés.
¿Dónde vives?Vivo en Madrid.
¿A qué te dedicas?Soy ingeniero. · Trabajo en un banco.
¿Cuántos años tienes?Tengo treinta años.
¿A qué te dedicas? is the normal way to ask what someone does. Translating «what is your job» as ¿cuál es tu trabajo? is understandable but nobody says it.

2. Four verbs do all the work

You already met these. Here they are doing something useful.

verbwhat forexample
serwho you are, where you are from, what you doSoy Brandon. Soy de Londres. Soy ingeniero.
vivir enwhere you live nowVivo en Madrid.
trabajar en / dethe place / the roleTrabajo en un banco. Trabajo de camarero.
tenerage, familyTengo treinta años. Tengo dos hermanos.
No «a» in front of your job. English says «I am an engineer». Spanish says Soy ingeniero — nothing in between. Soy un ingeniero sounds like you are one specimen of a species.

3. The age trap, again

This one comes back from the frío class, and it catches everyone twice.

you want to saywrongright
I’m thirty.Soy treinta.Tengo treinta años.
I’m hungry.Soy hambre.Tengo hambre.
I’m cold.Soy frío.Tengo frío.
And the años is not optional. Tengo treinta on its own means nothing — thirty what? The word has to be there.

4. Family, and your status

The second round of questions, once the first five are done.

SpanishEnglish
Tengo un hermano y una hermana.I have a brother and a sister.
No tengo hermanos.I don’t have any brothers or sisters.
Estoy casado. · Estoy soltero.I’m married. · I’m single.
Tengo dos hijos.I have two children.
No tengo hijos.I don’t have children.
Married and single go with estar, not ser. It is a state you are in, and it can change — which is exactly what estar is for. You will see the whole rule in the next class.

5. A normal day, in five lines

You do not need any new grammar for this — just the present you already have. Keep it to five sentences and you will be able to say it out loud without stopping.

— Por la mañana trabajo en casa.

— Como a las dos.

— Por la tarde voy al gimnasio.

— Ceno sobre las nueve.

— Los fines de semana veo a mis amigos.

Por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche. Not en la mañana — that is Latin America. In Spain it is por.

6. Now you talk

The last stretch, out loud. I ask, you answer — and then we swap and you ask me.

  1. The five questions, in order. Answer each one in a full sentence, not one word.
  2. Now ask me the same five. Change me llamo to ¿cómo te llamas? and so on.
  3. Tell me about your family in three sentences.
  4. Your normal Tuesday, in five sentences. Use por la mañana and por la tarde.
  5. Last one: say three things about yourself that are not true. I have to guess which ones.

Homework

Record yourself — just your phone, one minute — answering the five questions and describing your normal day. Send it to me. Do not write it down first.
