The present, and the ones that break it

Three endings to learn once, and then the handful of verbs that refuse to follow them — which are, of course, the ones you need most.

1. The regular present, in one table

Take off the -ar, -er or -ir, and add these. That is the whole rule.

hablar (-AR)comer (-ER)vivir (-IR)
yohablocomovivo
túhablascomesvives
él / ella / ustedhablacomevive
nosotroshablamoscomemosvivimos
vosotroshabláiscoméisvivís
ellos / ellashablancomenviven
Look at the -ER and -IR columns. They are identical except for nosotros and vosotros. So it is really two sets of endings, not three.

2. The ones that only break in «yo»

These behave perfectly everywhere except the first person. Learn the yo and you have learnt the verb.

3. The ones that change in the middle

The vowel in the stem breaks — but never in nosotros and vosotros. Those two always stay regular. That is the pattern, and it holds for dozens of verbs.

changeverbyo, tú, él, ellosnosotros / vosotros
e → iequererquiero, quieres…queremos, queréis
e → ieempezarempiezo…empezamos, empezáis
o → uepoderpuedo, puedes…podemos, podéis
o → uedormirduermo…dormimos, dormís
e → ipedirpido, pides…pedimos, pedís
The shape is a boot. Draw a line round the forms that change and you get a boot: everything except nosotros and vosotros. Every verb in this group does the same thing.

4. The four that do whatever they want

No pattern, no excuses. These four are the most used verbs in the language, so you will have them by heart within a week whether you study them or not.
